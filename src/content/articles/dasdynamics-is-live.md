I have wanted this for years. Today it is online.

Dynamics has been my job since 2009, and along the way it became much more than a job: a good part of my life. I wrote about it on a blog, then on LinkedIn. Both did their part, but neither was really mine. A blog ages, a feed scrolls away.

dasdynamics.dev is the place I wanted: mine, personal, built to last. A site, a name and a logo, a small D inside a larger one. D as Dynamics. D as Diego. It may look like a small thing, but publishing it today is a real thrill.

## Why

The reason is the same one that has kept me writing all these years: giving something back to the community I learned from. Concrete things, tried on real projects.

Here you find the articles I published on LinkedIn, kept up to date, and my old AX notes, each one re-read against today's D365FO with a verdict on whether it still holds. New articles will be published here first.

## Where I have been

Lately I have published less, and there is a reason. I am deep into AI projects inside my company. They are not about the product: they are about how we run the lifecycle of a project. For now I can't write much about them. So yes, on the product side I have been a bit out of the game.

I have not disappeared. I follow the community, I join every event I can, and in December I will be in Miami for [Convergence 2026](/articles/convergence-2026-miami/). I am just very busy on the AI front.

## From here

For me this site is a point of arrival, and a new start. Begin from the [articles](/articles/), follow the [RSS feed](/rss.xml) if you like, and if you spot something wrong or out of date, tell me on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/diego-mancassola-81a9a11b/).
